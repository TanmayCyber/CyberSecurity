## DAY 1 – DAY 3

I was so excited to start learning cybersecurity on my first day that I didn't even take a bath. I just sat down at my study table with my laptop and started learning.

Like most beginners, I wasn't sure where to begin. Almost every AI I talked to recommended that I start with **Networking** before moving on to **Linux**, so I followed that advice.

I spent the day watching **2–3 YouTube videos** covering the basics of networking to get a rough idea of how computer networks work. Then I continued doing the same for the next **2 more days**. By the end of **Day 3**, I had watched more than **10 videos**.

## DAY 4

After spending **3 days** trying to learn Networking, I understood some concepts like the **CIA Triad** for security and the **DAD Triad** for attacking.
But then I started getting confused and began to wonder whether what I was learning was even useful.
This doubt came to my mind because almost every YouTuber had different content for networking basics.

So, I decieded to skip Networking for now and decieded to start learning Linux.

Then a similar problem came up — every YouTube tutorial skipped the basics, and the ones that did teach Linux basics used terminology that I, as a beginner, couldn't understand.
In the process of trying to find good learning resources, I got to know about TryHackMe, GitHub, LabEx, and Reddit.
AI recommended that I document everything I learn on GitHub. But I thought I shouldn't just document whatever I learn — I should also document what I experienced.
So that's how my day ended.

## Day 5

I starting learning Linux from AI generated content/notes and doing hands on labs on LabEx, I also found that LabEx is a really great website for a beginner to start learning linux. I leared many commands like `cat` , `ls` , `cd` .

## Day 6 – Day 13

I created my first GitHub repository and started documenting everything I learned. However, I relied too much on AI, and almost everything in that repository was AI-generated. After a while, I realized that I wasn't actually learning much by doing that.

So, on **Day 9**, I deleted that repository and created this one. This time, I decided to write everything myself and use AI only to help me understand concepts, not to write my notes for me.

Looking back, deleting my first repository was one of the best decisions I made because it encouraged me to learn, understand, and explain things in my own words.
