## DAY 1 - DAY 3

On my first day I was so excited to start learning about Cybersecurity. I didn’t even have a bath. I just sat down at my study desk and started learning.
Like most people starting out, I wasn’t sure where to start. Most of the AIs I spoke to suggested to start with Networking and then move onto Linux.
I watched 2-3 networking videos on YouTube and got a basic understanding of how computer networks work. I spent the next 2 days doing the same thing. By the end of Day 3 I had watched over 10 videos.

## DAY 4


After 3 days of trying to learn Networking, I had a basic understanding of some concepts such as the CIA Triad for security and DAD Triad for attacking.
However I was getting confused, and started to doubt if I was learning anything useful.
This was because most YouTubers had different content for basics of Networking.
I decided to skip Networking and start learning Linux.
Again I had the same issue. All of the tutorials on YouTube skipped over the basics. Those who did teach the basics used terms I, as a beginner, couldn’t understand.
While trying to find some good resources to learn from, I discovered TryHackMe, GitHub, LabEx and Reddit.
The AI suggested that I should write everything I learned on GitHub. However, I thought I should not only write what I learned but what I had experienced. This is how my day finished.

## Day 5


I started learning Linux from AI generated content/notes. I also started doing hands on labs on LabEx. LabEx is a great resource for beginners. I learned lots of commands such as `cat`, `ls`, `cd`.

## Day 6 - Day 13


I created my first GitHub repository and started writing everything I was learning. However I was over relying on the AI. Everything in my repository was generated by AI, After some time I realised I wasn’t actually creating anything myselves.
So on Day 9 I deleted my repository and created this one. This time I decided to write everything myself. i decieded to use the AI to help me understand concepts but not to write for me.
Looking back, deleting my first repository was one of the best decisions I made. It motivated me to learn, understand and explain things in my own words.
